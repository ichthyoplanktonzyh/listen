# Phase 2.20 Research: English Listening, Rhythm, Prosody, And Reference Projects

状态：DRAFT

## Research Question

Phase 2.20 的核心判断是：

> 英语听力里，用户首先需要抓住真实语流中的重音节奏框架，再展开弱读音团和局部 phone evidence。

本调研用于确认这个判断是否站得住，并收集可参考的研究、数据集、工具和产品方向。

## Short Answer

我们的方向基本正确，但需要更精确地表述：

- 正确：真实英语听力不是逐 phone 等权解析。学习者首先需要识别 prominence、stress anchors、
  weak/reduced material、pause/phrase boundaries 和 compressed spans。
- 正确：phone-level evidence 很重要，但更适合做解释层和局部证据层，而不是默认主视图。
- 需要修正：不要把 “English is stress-timed” 当作严格物理定律。更稳的产品表述是
  “English listeners rely heavily on prosodic prominence, stress cues, duration, pauses and
  reductions for segmentation and meaning.”
- 需要修正：RhythmFrame 第一版不应声称已经检测完整 intonation/prosody。它应从可验证的
  lexical stress、word timing、duration、pause、function-word grouping 和 connected-speech
  evidence 开始。

## Why Rhythm-First Is Plausible

### 1. Speech perception is not phone-by-phone decoding

English listeners use stress and prosodic cues for segmentation. The classic metrical segmentation
line of work argues that English listeners treat strong syllables as likely lexical onsets and use
rhythmic/prosodic structure to find words in continuous speech.

Reference:

- Cutler & Norris, “The role of strong syllables in segmentation for lexical access”, 1988.
  Public copy: <https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_68854/component/file_503903/content>
- Cutler, Mehler, Norris & Segui, “A language-specific comprehension strategy”, Nature, 1983.
  DOI page: <https://doi.org/10.1038/304159a0>

Product implication:

- A listening UI should not start from an undifferentiated phone stream.
- It should first expose likely anchors and segmentation cues.

### 2. L2 listeners often struggle with reduced/connected speech, not only missing words

Connected speech, weak forms and reductions are repeatedly treated in L2 listening/pronunciation
research as major reasons learners fail to recognize known words in fluent speech.

Reference directions:

- Field, “Promoting perception: lexical segmentation in L2 listening”, ELT Journal, 2003.
  DOI page: <https://doi.org/10.1093/elt/57.4.325>
- Brown & Hilferty, “The effectiveness of teaching reduced forms for listening comprehension”,
  RELC Journal, 1986. DOI page: <https://doi.org/10.1177/003368828601700104>
- A recent open-access overview touching reduced forms and connected-speech perception:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076711/>

Product implication:

- The user-facing question should be “which words got backgrounded/compressed/linked?” before
  “which exact phone did the model hear?”

### 3. Prosody carries prominence, phrasing and meaning

Prosody is a real linguistic layer, not a UI decoration. Prominence, boundary strength, pitch
movement, duration and pause all contribute to how speech is segmented and understood.

Reference:

- ToBI prosodic annotation ecosystem:
  <https://lingtools.uoregon.edu/tobi/>
- Boston University Radio News Corpus, a reference English corpus with prosodic annotation:
  <https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC96S36>
- “The Buckeye Corpus of Conversational Speech”, project page:
  <https://buckeyecorpus.osu.edu/>

Product implication:

- RhythmFrame should be a first-class sound-analysis view.
- Phrase boundaries and stress anchors deserve UI priority equal to or higher than phone details.

### 4. Phone evidence is still valuable, but current PER limits default use

Our Phase 2.19 initial scoring shows the current phone layer is useful for evidence, but not yet
stable enough to be the default teaching truth:

- TED-LIUM transcript/timing import is stable.
- Some Buckeye windows are plausible.
- TIMIT Phase 2.17 artifact is much worse than historical baseline and needs window/mapping/model
  investigation.

Product implication:

- Raw CTC phone output should not drive the primary learner display.
- It can strengthen or weaken hotspots, expose uncertainty and support advanced inspection.

## Research Caveats

### Stress-timed English is useful but oversimplified

The common classroom distinction “English is stress-timed” is pedagogically useful, but modern
phonetics usually treats rhythm as gradient and measurable through timing variability, not as a
simple binary class.

Useful reference direction:

- Ramus, Nespor & Mehler, “Correlates of linguistic rhythm in the speech signal”, Cognition, 1999.
  DOI page: <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(99)00058-X>
- Grabe & Low, “Durational variability in speech and the rhythm class hypothesis”, 2002.
  DOI page: <https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110197105.2.515>

Product wording:

- Prefer “stress/rhythm/prominence frame” over “stress-timed truth.”
- Mark duration-only rhythm claims as tentative unless supported by stronger evidence.

### Pitch/F0 is useful but not required for v0

Pitch movement and pitch accent matter, but a first product baseline can start with:

- lexical stress
- word/phone timing
- pause/gap
- duration/compression
- function-word grouping
- connected-speech markers

F0/energy should be a follow-up enhancement after v0 resource shape, UI and QA are stable.

## Reference Tools And Projects

### Praat

Purpose:

- acoustic analysis, spectrogram, pitch, intensity, formants, TextGrid annotation

Why relevant:

- Best reference tool for manual prosody/phonetics inspection.
- Useful for validating whether a suspected stress anchor or boundary has acoustic evidence.

Link:

- <https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/>

### Montreal Forced Aligner

Purpose:

- forced alignment from audio + transcript to word/phone timing, often exported as TextGrid

Why relevant:

- Strong reference for word/phone boundary generation in production pipelines.
- Helps separate timing quality from CTC phone-recognition quality.

Link:

- <https://montreal-forced-aligner.readthedocs.io/>

### ToBI / Prosodic Annotation

Purpose:

- annotation system for tones, break indices, pitch accents and prosodic structure

Why relevant:

- Provides a mature vocabulary for prominence and phrase boundaries.
- We should not implement full ToBI now, but its concepts can guide RhythmFrame terms.

Links:

- <https://lingtools.uoregon.edu/tobi/>
- <https://www.ling.ohio-state.edu/research/phonetics/E_ToBI/>

### AuToBI

Purpose:

- automatic ToBI-style prosodic annotation toolkit

Why relevant:

- Historical reference for automatic pitch accent / boundary detection features.
- Useful for later F0/energy/pitch-accent work, not required for Phase 2.20 v0.

Links:

- <https://github.com/AndrewRosenberg/AuToBI>
- AuToBI paper/project page: <https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~agus/tobi/>

### ProsodyPro

Purpose:

- Praat script for large-scale prosody analysis

Why relevant:

- Practical reference for extracting F0/duration/intensity measurements once we add acoustic
  prosody features.

Link:

- <https://sites.google.com/site/prosodypro/>

### SPPAS

Purpose:

- phonetic annotation and alignment toolkit

Why relevant:

- Reference for reproducible annotation pipelines and TextGrid-like workflows.

Link:

- <https://sppas.org/>

### openSMILE

Purpose:

- acoustic feature extraction toolkit

Why relevant:

- Candidate future feature extractor for energy, pitch-related features and prosody evidence if
  we decide not to hand-roll acoustic extraction.

Link:

- <https://audeering.github.io/opensmile/>

### Parselmouth

Purpose:

- Python access to Praat functionality

Why relevant:

- Lightweight bridge for future production-side pitch/intensity extraction without driving the
  Praat GUI.

Link:

- <https://parselmouth.readthedocs.io/>

## Reference Datasets

### TIMIT

Use:

- clean phone/boundary evidence
- lexical stress sanity through transcript/pronunciation

Limit:

- read speech, not ideal for natural connected speech or weak-form richness.

### Buckeye

Use:

- natural conversational reductions, weak forms, real pronunciation, timing

Limit:

- noisy windows, disfluencies, special tags and lead-ins require careful filtering.

Link:

- <https://buckeyecorpus.osu.edu/>

### TED-LIUM

Use:

- long-form transcript/timing and product-like public speech

Limit:

- no phone-level gold; rhythm QA needs manual annotation.

Link:

- <https://aclanthology.org/L12-1405/>
- <https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/tedlium>

### Boston University Radio News Corpus

Use:

- English broadcast speech with prosodic labels; useful reference for pitch accent / phrase
  boundary concepts.

Limit:

- Licensing/access may require LDC.

Link:

- <https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC96S36>

### Switchboard Prosody / ToBI-Style Resources

Use:

- conversational English prosody and discourse-level prominence, if available locally or through
  licensed datasets.

Limit:

- access/licensing complexity.

Reference direction:

- <https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/>

## Product/UX Reference Directions

### YouGlish / PlayPhrase-like search

Use:

- product pattern for learning from real clips.

Relevance:

- Good reference for corpus playback and repeated exposure, but they usually do not explain
  rhythm/prosody deeply. LLPlayerNext can differentiate by adding RhythmFrame and hotspots.

Links:

- <https://youglish.com/>
- <https://www.playphrase.me/>

### ELSA / Speechace / EnglishCentral-like pronunciation tools

Use:

- references for learner-facing pronunciation feedback, stress/intonation UI and scoring language.

Relevance:

- They are mostly speaking/pronunciation products, not listening-diagnosis products. Useful as UI
  comparison, but LLPlayerNext should stay listening-first.

Links:

- <https://elsaspeak.com/>
- <https://www.speechace.com/>
- <https://www.englishcentral.com/>

### BBC / British Council Connected Speech Teaching

Use:

- learner-facing vocabulary for weak forms, linking, assimilation, elision and rhythm.

Relevance:

- Good wording references for explanations; not algorithmic references.

Links:

- <https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/>
- <https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/>

## Implications For LLPlayerNext

### Pipeline

Phase 2.20 v0 should not wait for a better phone model.

Recommended first implementation:

```text
transcript words + WordTimeline
  -> expected pronunciation + lexical stress
  -> function/content word classification
  -> pause/gap and duration features
  -> connected-speech markers as optional evidence
  -> RhythmFrame
```

Phone evidence should enter as:

- confidence modifier
- hotspot support
- advanced detail
- error attribution signal

### UI

Default display should answer:

```text
What should I catch?
What is backgrounded?
Where is it compressed?
Where does the sound break?
Why does it not sound like the dictionary form?
```

Phone view should answer:

```text
What is the local evidence?
Which expected sounds are reduced/deleted/merged?
How reliable is the model here?
```

### Evaluation

Evaluation should be layered:

1. text/timing integrity
2. phone evidence quality
3. stress anchor quality
4. weak/compressed span quality
5. listening explanation usefulness

Do not use PER as the only go/no-go metric.

## Recommended Immediate Actions

1. Keep Phase 2.20 centered on RhythmFrame v0, not phone-model replacement.
2. Add manual QA labels for stress anchors, weak groups, compression spans and listening hotspots
   before expanding many more cases.
3. Use TIMIT and Buckeye for phone evidence, but add manually scored product/TED-LIUM segments for
   rhythm explanation.
4. Avoid full ToBI/F0 implementation in v0. Treat ToBI/Praat/AuToBI as design references and future
   enhancement paths.
5. Add a small pronunciation-provider stress extraction audit before coding RhythmFrame, because
   lexical stress quality becomes more important than raw phone labels in this direction.
